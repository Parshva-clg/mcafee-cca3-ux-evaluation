# Qualitative analysis

Evidence consists of five supplied pairs of comments, not independently verified verbatim audio transcripts. Each supplied pair describes one focus. All five favour the redesign; negative prototype feedback was not supplied. Uniform direction may reflect selection, prompting or scripting. Participant recruitment, independence, consent, facilitation, sequence and task equivalence have not been established. Earlier project context includes prepared think-aloud scripts; spontaneous speech cannot be assumed. Audio files are archived unchanged and have not been compared with the text.

## Themes and evidence

### P1 Navigation

Original: Many categories; uncertain starting point. Prototype: Named top links make destination clearer. Artifact check: Matching navigation labels are present.

### P2 Feature understanding

Original: Dense feature descriptions. Prototype: Separate feature cards and demonstration help comprehension. Artifact check: Feature cards and illustrative device/privacy/identity tabs are present.

### P3 Plan comparison

Original: Hard to compare inclusions and choose. Prototype: Side-by-side plans feel easier to compare. Artifact check: Plan cards and overview table are present. Individual/family switching is NOT present; preserve but do not validate that claim.

### P4 Attention and readability

Original: Competing promotional and product content. Prototype: Headings, spacing, colour and motion feel clearer. Artifact check: Styles support these design intentions; no measured reading speed or accessibility outcome.

### P5 Support and reassurance

Original: Extra searching for refund and support details. Prototype: Guarantee and expandable answers feel discoverable. Artifact check: Guarantee banner, support link and refund FAQ are present.

## Interpretation

Reduced perceived information overload is a cross-cutting interpretation of P1–P4, rather than a separate measured outcome. Navigation, explanation, comparison, readability and reassurance each have direct support from one labelled pair. Do not report each individual theme as observed in all five participants. Supplied comments describe perceived benefits; no task completion rates, elapsed times, click counts or error rates can be recovered from them.

P3's individual/family switch claim conflicts with the actual HTML, whose selector offers All-round protection and My devices. The quote is retained without editing. Plan price comparison and a complete purchase journey are not implemented: offer links lead externally and live prices are absent.

## Study limitations

Five people would provide exploratory feedback, not representative validation. Five short topic-specific excerpts do not establish that every person completed the same core task on both versions. An original-first order, if used as instructed, introduces learning/order effects. The attached prototype is HTML, while the assignment asks for Figma; no Figma URL was supplied. Full interaction accessibility and real security functionality were not tested.
